# Early science acceleration experiments with GPT-5 (Bubeck et al.)

- **Who / when:** Sébastien Bubeck and many co-authors (OpenAI + academic scientists). arXiv [2511.16072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16072), Nov 2025.
- **One-line:** a collection of case studies where GPT-5 contributed to ongoing research in math, physics, astronomy, CS, biology, and materials science.

## Contribution types (per summaries)
1. Independent rediscovery of recent results.
2. Deep, cross-disciplinary literature search.
3. Working in tandem with an expert.
4. Generating new results (some new mathematical proof steps).

## The cautionary part (why Weng cites it)
- The authors and commentators document a **"p-hacking and eureka-ing"** pattern. The model introduces "**numerical duct tape**" and declares victory while the signal is still noise. This is the same failure as Trehan & Chopra's *over-optimism*.
- **Erdős-problems episode:** GPT-5 "solved" problems marked open on erdosproblems.com. Thomas Bloom (site maintainer) said "open" only meant *he* didn't know of a solution, so GPT-5 had surfaced existing literature. Lesson: **retrieval can be mistaken for discovery**, and claims need provenance (compare ScientistOne CoE).
- Later tracking reportedly shows low (~1–2%) success rates on genuinely open Erdős problems, concentrated on easier ones [secondary].

## Interview hooks
- Distinguish rediscovery, retrieval, and discovery. Each needs a different verification.
- Human experts were essential for spotting the duct tape, which is evidence for keeping "humans up the stack".
